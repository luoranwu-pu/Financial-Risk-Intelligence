## Eligibility principles for high-quality liquid assets

High-quality liquid assets are assets that can be converted into cash at little or no loss of value under the LCR stress scenario. BCBS 238 and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61 both require the buffer to be unencumbered, under the control of the liquidity management function, and free of legal, contractual, or operational obstacles to monetisation. Assets issued by the institution itself are excluded. The buffer is not an accounting portfolio: encumbered securities, including those pledged and not available for use, do not count as HQLA. Eligibility is assessed in stress, so a deep market in normal times is not enough if the asset cannot be monetised in the combined idiosyncratic and market-wide scenario.

## Level 1 assets and the zero haircut

Level 1 assets generally carry a zero haircut and are not subject to the Level 2 composition cap. The Level 1 set includes coins and banknotes, withdrawable central bank reserves, and marketable claims on or guaranteed by sovereigns, central banks, and certain public entities that qualify for a zero risk weight, subject to the conditions in BCBS 238 and in the EU LCR Delegated Act. Domestic-currency sovereign debt can also qualify where the liquidity risk is taken in that currency, even when the risk weight is not zero. Because Level 1 assets are the core of the buffer, a position concentrated in Level 2 assets can look adequate after haircuts and still be capped out of the ratio.

## Level 2A assets and the 15 percent haircut

Level 2A assets are recognised after a 15 percent haircut. Under the Basel LCR standard, Level 2A includes marketable securities issued or guaranteed by sovereigns and public entities with a 20 percent risk weight, and qualifying corporate debt securities and covered bonds rated at least AA minus, not issued by a financial institution or an affiliate. Level 2 assets together, after haircuts, are capped at 40 percent of the total HQLA stock. The 15 percent haircut and the 40 percent Level 2 cap are separate constraints: the haircut reduces the value that enters the stock, and the cap can exclude the surplus above 40 percent.

## Level 2B assets and the 15 percent composition cap

Level 2B assets are subject to higher haircuts and to a 15 percent composition cap of total HQLA. In the Basel standard, eligible residential mortgage-backed securities that meet the additional conditions take a 25 percent haircut, while certain corporate debt securities rated between A plus and BBB minus, and certain common equity shares, take a 50 percent haircut. Level 2B is a national-discretion category in Basel and a tightly specified category in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61. The 15 percent cap applies after haircuts and sits inside the broader 40 percent cap on all Level 2 assets. Assets that miss the eligibility conditions stay at a zero recognised value, not at a discretionary haircut.

## Operational requirements for the liquidity buffer

The HQLA buffer must be operationally available throughout the 30 calendar day horizon. BCBS 238 requires the assets to be managed as a liquidity buffer, with periodic monetisation tests so that access to the market is real. Diversification should be consistent with the bank's funding and collateral profile. Currency consistency matters: liquid assets are expected to be convertible into the currency of the net outflow, allowing for the treatment of HQLA surplus in other currencies. A buffer that is Level 1 in form but blocked by settlement, pledge, or transfer restrictions is not available HQLA for the LCR.
