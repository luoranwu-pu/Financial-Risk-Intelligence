## EBA guidelines on institutions stress testing

EBA/GL/2018/04 is the EBA Guidelines on institutions' stress testing. The guidelines set expectations for the governance and design of stress-testing programmes, including liquidity stress testing. They apply across EU institutions as guidelines under the EBA's mandate. They are not the ECB Guide to the ILAAP, and they are not the binding formula for the LCR in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61. A comparison of "ECB versus EBA" liquidity expectations is a comparison of an ILAAP supervisory guide with a stress-testing guideline, read against the binding CRR framework.

## Liquidity scenarios and multiple horizons

EBA/GL/2018/04 expects liquidity stress tests to use several scenarios and more than one time horizon, aligned to the institution's liquidity risk profile. The scenarios should include severe but plausible stresses and should reflect funding concentrations, collateral calls, and market constraints on monetising assets. The 30 calendar day LCR horizon is one regulatory point. It is not the only horizon the guidelines expect a programme to cover. Scenario design should be documented well enough that a second-line reviewer can see which assumptions drive the outflow.

## Interaction between solvency and liquidity

EBA/GL/2018/04 expects institutions to capture the interaction between solvency and liquidity stress. Rating migration, collateral haircuts, and a rise in funding costs can hit capital and liquidity together. A liquidity scenario that holds capital resources constant, or a solvency scenario that ignores the funding of new collateral outflows, misses that interaction. The guideline therefore pushes programmes beyond a standalone LCR recalculation. Second-line review should ask whether the liquidity stress changes capital, and whether the capital stress changes the funding plan.

## From stress results to limits and the contingency funding plan

EBA/GL/2018/04 expects stress-testing results to feed the risk appetite, the limit framework, and the contingency funding plan. Results that are reported and then archived do not meet that use test. Early warning indicators should be consistent with the scenarios that would trigger the plan. Management actions in the plan should be feasible under the same stress that creates the shortfall, including a market-wide stress in which wholesale markets are not open. The guideline's emphasis is on programme design and on the use of results, rather than on defining an internal survival horizon as the ECB ILAAP Guide does.

## Reverse stress testing

EBA/GL/2018/04 expects reverse stress testing as part of the programme. For liquidity, reverse stress testing asks which outflow, haircut, or market closure would exhaust liquidity resources, including resources above the LCR minimum. The exercise is a search for breaking points. It is not a forecast and it is not a capital-planning base case. Outcomes should inform risk appetite when the breaking point sits inside a plausible range. Reverse stress testing complements, and does not replace, the severe but plausible scenarios run forward from today's balance sheet.
