## Role of the ILAAP in ECB supervision

The ECB Guide to the ILAAP sets supervisory expectations for the internal liquidity adequacy assessment process of significant institutions. The ILAAP is the bank's own process for identifying, measuring, and managing liquidity risk, and for demonstrating that liquidity is adequate. It is an internal process that feeds supervision. It is not a second copy of the LCR report. The Guide expects the ILAAP to be decision-useful for the management body and to give the joint supervisory team a clear view of liquidity risk appetite, internal stress, and management actions. Public legal minima still come from the CRR and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61.

## Liquidity risk appetite and internal limits

The ECB expects the management body to set a liquidity risk appetite that is specific, approved, and translated into limits and early warning indicators. Internal limits should sit on the regulatory ratios and on internal metrics, including a survival horizon under the bank's own stress. A limit framework that only restates the 100 percent LCR minimum does not meet the intention of an internal appetite. The appetite should state what the bank will not accept, such as reliance on a single funding market or an intraday shortfall, and it should be consistent with the contingency funding plan. Breach governance, including who is informed and which actions are available, is part of the appetite, not an annex.

## Stress testing beyond the 30-day ratio

The ECB Guide expects liquidity stress testing under idiosyncratic, market-wide, and combined scenarios, across several time horizons, including horizons longer than the 30-day LCR window. The standardised LCR scenario remains the regulatory ratio. Internal scenarios are expected to use the bank's own funding concentrations, currency mix, and business model. Results should change management actions and limits when they show a shortfall. A stress framework that only reproduces the LCR weights at a 30 calendar day point is not the internal test the Guide describes.

## Survival horizon and internal buffers

The ECB Guide expects institutions to estimate an internal survival horizon and to hold internal liquidity buffers where the risk profile requires resources above the regulatory minimum. The survival horizon is the period the bank can meet outflows under its internal stress before management actions, and it should also be shown with credible management actions. The horizon is an internal risk metric. It is not a number defined as a single EU legal minimum in the LCR Delegated Act. Using the LCR of 100 percent as if it were the survival horizon confuses a standardised 30 calendar day ratio with an internal duration of resilience.

## Governance data and SREP use

The ECB expects ILAAP data to be accurate, aggregated in time for decisions, and traceable to risk and finance sources. The management body is expected to challenge the ILAAP, not only to receive it. ILAAP conclusions are an input to the supervisory review and evaluation process. Weaknesses in stress testing, risk appetite, or data can lead to qualitative measures and can inform additional liquidity requirements. The Guide therefore links internal process quality to SREP outcomes. It does not replace EBA stress-testing guidelines, which set methodological expectations for the stress-testing programme itself.
