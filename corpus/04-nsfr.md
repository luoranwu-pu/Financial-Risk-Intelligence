## The one-year stable funding requirement

The NSFR stress horizon is one year. BCBS 295 defines the net stable funding ratio as available stable funding divided by required stable funding, and the minimum NSFR is 100 percent. Regulation (EU) No 575/2013, as amended by CRR2, implements that one-year structural requirement in Articles 428a and following. The NSFR asks whether the asset side and off-balance-sheet commitments are funded with liabilities that are still expected to stay over a year of idiosyncratic stress. It is not a 30 calendar day test and it does not use the LCR inflow cap.

## Available stable funding factors

Available stable funding is the portion of capital and liabilities treated as reliable over the one-year horizon. Regulatory capital and liabilities with a residual maturity of one year or more receive a 100 percent ASF factor. Stable retail and small-business deposits receive a 95 percent ASF factor. Less stable retail and small-business deposits receive a 90 percent ASF factor. Wholesale funding from non-financial corporates with residual maturity below one year receives a 50 percent ASF factor. Short-term funding from financial institutions, and other liabilities with residual maturity below six months, generally receive a 0 percent ASF factor. These factors are the Basel standard weights. The EU articles specify the binding European calibration.

## Required stable funding factors

Required stable funding reflects how much stable funding an asset or commitment needs. Coins, banknotes, and central bank reserves require 0 percent RSF. Unencumbered Level 1 securities require 5 percent RSF. Unencumbered Level 2A assets require 15 percent RSF. Unencumbered Level 2B assets require 50 percent RSF. Residential mortgages with a residual maturity of at least one year and a qualifying low risk weight require 65 percent RSF. Other loans to non-financial customers with residual maturity of at least one year require 85 percent RSF. Encumbered assets are assigned an RSF factor by the longer of the encumbrance period and the asset maturity. Off-balance-sheet commitments also receive RSF factors.

## How NSFR complements the LCR

The LCR and the NSFR are complementary, not substitutes. The LCR is a 30 calendar day coverage ratio of HQLA over net cash outflows. The NSFR is a one-year ratio of available stable funding over required stable funding. A bank can hold a high LCR, built on short-dated central bank reserves and deposits that run off slowly in a 30-day weight, and still have a weak NSFR if those liabilities are not stable over a year. The reverse is also possible. Liquidity risk appetite should set limits on both ratios. Supervisory dialogue under ILAAP looks at the internal view of funding risk beyond either single minimum.
