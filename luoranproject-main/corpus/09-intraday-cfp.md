## Intraday liquidity risk

Intraday liquidity risk is the risk that payments cannot be made at the time they are due, even if the end-of-day LCR is above 100 percent. Directive 2013/36/EU Article 86 explicitly includes intraday horizons in liquidity risk management. Basel monitoring of intraday liquidity looks at available intraday liquidity at the start of the day, the daily maximum usage, time-specific obligations, and payments made and received. The LCR does not measure this risk because its horizon is 30 calendar days and its weights are end-of-day stressed flows. A surplus HQLA portfolio that cannot be turned into central bank balances during the payment day does not solve an intraday shortfall.

## Contingency funding plans

A contingency funding plan sets the actions, responsibilities, and funding sources the bank will use when a stress moves from early warning to a declared contingency. EBA/GL/2018/04 expects stress results to feed that plan. The ECB ILAAP Guide expects the plan to be consistent with the liquidity risk appetite. Actions should be classified by the time they take and by whether they remain available in a market-wide stress. A plan that relies only on unsecured wholesale issuance is weak against the same scenario the LCR already treats as stressed. The plan should also say how the LCR and the internal survival horizon are monitored while the contingency is open.

## Early warning indicators

Early warning indicators should signal a deterioration before a limit is breached. Useful indicators include a fall in the LCR buffer above 100 percent, a rise in funding concentration, wider funding spreads from the market-related monitoring tools, and higher intraday usage. EBA/GL/2018/04 links indicators to the scenarios that would trigger the contingency funding plan. Indicators that are not tied to a decision, or that are reviewed only after the monthly LCR production cycle, do not function as early warnings. Second-line review should test whether an indicator has an owner and a threshold.

## How intraday risk enters the ILAAP

The ECB Guide expects the ILAAP to cover material liquidity risks, which includes intraday risk for any institution with significant payment or settlement activity. The internal view should state the sources of intraday liquidity, the time-specific obligations, and the management actions if a payment system or correspondent bank fails. This is separate from demonstrating a 100 percent LCR. An ILAAP pack that shows only the regulatory ratio and a one-year NSFR has not described intraday risk. CRD Article 86 is the legal governance anchor, and the ECB Guide is the supervisory expectation for how that risk appears in the internal assessment.
