## CRR Part Six liquidity requirements

Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 Part Six contains the EU liquidity requirements. Article 412 requires institutions to cover stressed net liquidity outflows with liquid assets over thirty days. Article 413 sets the stable funding requirement. Article 414 deals with failure to meet the liquidity requirements, including prompt notification to the competent authority and a plan to restore compliance. Article 415 requires liquidity reporting. The CRR is a regulation and is directly applicable. A briefing on "CRR liquidity" that stops at the LCR and ignores stable funding and the breach process in Article 414 is incomplete.

## CRD liquidity risk governance

Directive 2013/36/EU Article 86 requires robust strategies, policies, processes, and systems for the identification, measurement, management, and monitoring of liquidity risk over an appropriate set of time horizons, including intraday, so that institutions maintain adequate liquidity buffers. The CRD also requires alternative scenarios on liquidity positions and a contingency plan. Because the CRD is a directive, it is implemented through national law, while the technical liquidity ratios sit in the CRR and in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61. Governance failures under Article 86 can exist even when the reported LCR is above 100 percent.

## The LCR Delegated Regulation

Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61 specifies the liquidity coverage requirement that Article 412 of the CRR obliges institutions to meet. It defines liquid assets, outflow and inflow rates, the 75 percent inflow cap, and the calculation of the ratio. Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1620 amends parts of that specification. For EU legal interpretation, the Delegated Act is the operational LCR text. BCBS 238 remains the Basel standard and is the right citation for the international calibration, but it is not the EU legal instrument.

## NSFR in CRR2

CRR2 inserted the net stable funding ratio into Regulation (EU) No 575/2013, in particular Articles 428a and following. The minimum NSFR is 100 percent and the horizon is one year. Available stable funding and required stable funding are defined in those articles rather than in the LCR Delegated Act. A European compliance map should therefore point NSFR questions to CRR2 articles and to BCBS 295, and LCR questions to Article 412 and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61.

## EBA guidelines sitting under the rulebook

EBA guidelines do not replace the CRR or a delegated regulation. EBA/GL/2018/04 sets expectations for stress-testing programmes. Other EBA guidelines and technical standards cover reporting and disclosure of liquidity. Where this desk cites an EBA guideline, the citation is to supervisory methodology. Where it cites Article 412 or Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61, the citation is to a binding obligation. Mixing those layers is a common source of unsupported regulatory claims.
