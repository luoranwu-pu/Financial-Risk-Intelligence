## Five Basel liquidity monitoring tools

BCBS 238 complements the LCR with five liquidity monitoring tools. They are the contractual maturity mismatch, the concentration of funding, available unencumbered assets, the LCR by significant currency, and market-related monitoring tools. The tools are supervisory monitoring metrics. They are not a second minimum ratio and they do not change the 100 percent LCR floor. Their role is to show concentrations and mismatches that a single 30 calendar day ratio can hide. An ILAAP that reports only the headline LCR should still be able to produce these views.

## Contractual maturity mismatch

The contractual maturity mismatch sets assets and liabilities into time buckets by contractual maturity, so that gaps are visible beyond the 30 calendar day LCR window. Contractual gaps are not behavioural gaps. Retail deposits that are contractually overnight but behaviourally stable will look like a large short-term outflow in this tool. The tool is still useful because it shows the legal maturity the bank would face if behaviour changed. It should be read next to behavioural assumptions, not instead of the LCR.

## Concentration of funding

The concentration of funding tool identifies sources that are large enough to matter if they leave. Concentration can sit in a single counterparty, a product, a currency, or a market. The LCR run-off rates do not cap concentration risk. A deposit book can attract low standardised run-off rates and still be dangerously concentrated. ECB ILAAP expectations on risk appetite are the natural place to turn a concentration metric into a limit. The monitoring tool itself does not set that limit.

## Available unencumbered assets

Available unencumbered assets describe collateral that could still be pledged or sold, including assets that are not eligible as HQLA. The metric is broader than the LCR buffer. Some unencumbered assets support funding capacity but do not enter the numerator of the LCR because they fail the HQLA conditions. Keeping the two stocks separate avoids a common reporting error: treating every unencumbered bond as Level 1. Haircuts, eligibility, and operational availability still have to be applied before an asset is called HQLA.

## LCR by significant currency and market monitoring

The LCR by significant currency shows whether the 30 calendar day buffer exists in the currencies in which outflows arise. A group LCR above 100 percent can hide a shortfall in one significant currency. BCBS 238 treats currency consistency as part of buffer quality. Market-related monitoring tools track market-wide signals such as equity prices, debt spreads, and funding premia that can warn of a deterioration before a deposit run-off is observed. These tools inform early warning indicators in the contingency funding plan. They are not inputs to the arithmetic of the regulatory LCR.
