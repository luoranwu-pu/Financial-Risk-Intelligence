## The net cash outflow formula

Total net cash outflow equals stressed outflows minus inflows that are eligible to be recognised. The liquidity coverage ratio is HQLA divided by that net cash outflow. Inflows are not subtracted in full. Under BCBS 238 and Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61, recognised inflows cannot exceed the inflow cap. The denominator is therefore a net figure, and dividing HQLA by gross outflows alone is not the LCR. A calculator that drops the cap, or that ignores inflows entirely, is not implementing the regulatory formula.

## The 75 percent inflow cap

The inflow cap is 75 percent of total expected cash outflows. Inflows above that cap are excluded from the LCR calculation. The cap implies that total net cash outflow is at least 25 percent of gross outflows, even when contractual inflows are very large. The 75 percent limit and the 25 percent residual are the same rule stated two ways, not two separate floors. Specialized business models can be subject to a different cap only where the legal text allows it. In the ordinary case used by this desk, the cap remains 75 percent.

## Retail deposit run-off rates

Stable retail deposits run off at 5 percent, or at 3 percent where the deposit guarantee scheme meets the additional criteria in the Basel standard and the EU LCR Delegated Act. Less stable retail deposits run off at 10 percent. Deposits from small business customers that are managed like retail deposits can receive the retail treatment. Higher rates apply to categories the legal text treats as more likely to leave, including some brokered or high-value deposits. These rates are standardised weights for the regulatory ratio. An internal stress test may apply a higher retail run-off if the bank's own evidence supports it, but it may not apply a lower rate than the rule requires for the official LCR.

## Wholesale funding run-off rates

Unsecured wholesale funding is weighted by the type of provider and by whether the deposit is operational. Operational deposits, generated by clearing, custody, or cash-management services, run off at 25 percent, or at 5 percent where the additional deposit-insurance conditions are met. Unsecured wholesale funding from non-financial corporates, sovereigns, central banks, and public sector entities that is not operational runs off at 40 percent, or at 20 percent where it is fully covered by a qualifying deposit guarantee. Unsecured wholesale funding from financial institutions and other entities outside those categories runs off at 100 percent. A 20 percent increase in wholesale outflows is a scenario overlay on top of these weights, not a change to the weights themselves.

## Secured funding and other outflow categories

Secured funding run-off depends on the collateral. Transactions secured by Level 1 assets can have a zero outflow rate. Transactions secured by Level 2A assets are generally weighted at 15 percent, and transactions secured by other collateral are weighted more heavily, up to 100 percent where the collateral is not HQLA. Additional outflow categories include collateral substitution, downgrade triggers, loss of funding on asset-backed paper, and undrawn credit and liquidity facilities. Facility rates differ for retail, corporate, and financial customers. Because these items can dominate the denominator of a dealer or custody business, an LCR explanation that mentions only deposit run-off is incomplete for those balance sheets.
