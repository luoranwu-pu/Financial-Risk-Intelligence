## The 30-day liquidity coverage requirement

The LCR stress horizon is 30 calendar days. Under BCBS 238, the liquidity coverage ratio compares the stock of high-quality liquid assets with total net cash outflows over the next 30 calendar days. Regulation (EU) No 575/2013 Article 412 carries the same horizon into EU law: institutions hold liquid assets sufficient to cover net liquidity outflows under stressed conditions over a period of thirty days. The ratio is a short-term resilience test, not a structural funding test. A desk using the LCR should therefore keep the 30 calendar day window explicit whenever a stress question is framed.

## The binding 100 percent minimum

The minimum LCR is 100 percent. BCBS 238 requires the stock of high-quality liquid assets to be at least equal to total net cash outflows over the stress horizon, and the EU LCR framework applies that minimum on an ongoing basis. An LCR below 100 percent means the buffer does not cover modelled net outflows and is a breach of the regulatory minimum, not merely a watch-list event. Supervisors may expect management buffers above 100 percent, but those add-ons are institution-specific and are not a lower minimum. This briefing does not treat an internal target as a substitute for the 100 percent floor.

## Combined idiosyncratic and market-wide stress

The LCR stress scenario combines an idiosyncratic shock with a market-wide shock. BCBS 238 designs the 30 calendar day scenario so that a firm-specific stress and a market stress arrive together. Typical modelled features include partial loss of retail deposits, partial loss of unsecured wholesale funding, a step-up in collateral outflows, and calls on liquidity facilities. The scenario is standardised for the regulatory ratio. It is not the bank's only liquidity stress test. Internal ILAAP scenarios are expected to explore shocks that the standardised LCR does not fully represent.

## EU implementation in CRR and the LCR Delegated Act

In the EU, the binding LCR specification sits in Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2015/61, made under Regulation (EU) No 575/2013. Article 412 of the CRR sets the obligation to cover stressed net outflows over thirty days. The Delegated Act specifies what counts as liquid assets, how outflows and inflows are weighted, and how the ratio is calculated. Later amendments, including Commission Delegated Regulation (EU) 2018/1620, adjust particular items. For a European bank, a question about "the LCR rule" should be answered from the CRR plus the LCR Delegated Act, with BCBS 238 as the Basel standard those texts implement.

## Limits of what the LCR measures

The LCR does not measure structural funding over one year, and it does not measure intraday payment capacity. It is a 30 calendar day ratio with standardised weights. It does not, by itself, set the internal survival horizon that ECB ILAAP expectations ask a bank to estimate under its own scenarios. It also does not replace the contingency funding plan. A result above 100 percent can coexist with concentration in funding, a weak intraday position, or a scenario in which outflows arrive faster than the standardised rates. Those limits are why liquidity supervision uses the LCR together with NSFR, monitoring tools, and internal stress testing.
